What it is

A long/flat timing overlay on QQQ that blends two independent signals with complementary weaknesses: a slow trend filter (50/200-day moving-average crossover with fast re-entry) and a fast momentum filter (CCI(40) band with a 200-day triangular-MA regime switch). The trend filter is blind to sudden crashes; the momentum filter is weak in long grinding bears — so combining them covers both.

Your QQQ weight is the average of two yes/no filters — 100% if both say "in," 50% if only one, 0% if neither:

  1. Trend filter (SMA fast-reentry): "in" while the 50-day average is above the 200-day; "out" after a death cross; back "in" when price reclaims its 50-day average (min ~10 trading days between switches).
  2. Momentum filter (CCI40 TMA-trigger): "in" when CCI(40) is above 0; "out" when CCI(40) falls below −100 (or below 0 while the 200-day triangular average is sloping down).

Each day, set your QQQ allocation to 0% / 50% / 100% per the two filters and hold the rest in cash. This changes more often — about 12 times per year, including the half-steps.

Bottom line

The smoothest ride and the most reliable drawdown control, with protection against fast and slow crashes. You give up ~1.9 pp/yr of return and accept fractional positions and 4× the turnover. Best if you're near drawing on the money (sequence-of-returns risk) or would otherwise panic-sell in a fast crash.

How to execute it

  • Daily routine: after the close, check the two filters and set your QQQ weight per the rule above. Trade at the next session. Changes cluster around market turns; check daily in volatile periods.
  • Where to hold the asset: QQQ, or QQQM (same index, lower 0.15% fee). Do not use leveraged ETFs (TQQQ) — leverage breaks the risk profile.
  • Where to hold cash: a money-market fund, T-bills, or HYSA earning about the T-bill rate. The backtest credits cash the actual 3-month T-bill yield (averaging ~2% since 1999); that yield is a small part of the return edge, and the drawdown protection does not depend on it.
  • Account & tax: at ~12 trades/year plus fractional rebalances this realizes many short-term events — strongly prefer a tax-advantaged account (IRA/Roth/401k).
  • Discipline: follow the rule mechanically. The hard moments are selling when the market still feels fine and re-buying while it still feels scary.

What to expect / when it disappoints

  • It cushions every crash type, including fast ones — when one filter is caught off-guard, the other has usually exited, so you're only half-exposed (COVID −21% vs −29% for buy & hold).
  • Its drawdown has been shallower than buy & hold in 89–90% of rolling 3–5 year windows — the most consistent risk reduction of anything tested.
  • The cost is real: ~12 changes/year and fractional (50%) positions to rebalance — more effort and more taxable events than the either-on blend.
  • In a relentless bull market you will trail buy & hold — the protection only pays off in the next deep bear.
