What it is

A long/flat timing overlay on QQQ that blends two independent signals with complementary weaknesses: a slow trend filter (50/200-day moving-average crossover with fast re-entry) and a fast momentum filter (CCI(40) band with a 200-day triangular-MA regime switch). The trend filter is blind to sudden crashes; the momentum filter is weak in long grinding bears — so combining them covers both.

You are in QQQ by default. You move to cash only when both of these say "out" at the same time, and you return to QQQ the moment either one says "in" again:

  1. Trend filter (SMA fast-reentry): "out" after a death cross (50-day average falls below the 200-day); back "in" when price closes above its 50-day average (min ~10 trading days between switches).
  2. Momentum filter (CCI40 TMA-trigger): "out" when CCI(40) falls below −100 (or below 0 while the 200-day triangular average is sloping down); back "in" when CCI(40) rises above 0.

Because you only sit out when both agree, exits are rare and brief — historically about 3 trades per year, and the position is always simply 100% QQQ or 100% cash.

Bottom line

Maximum terminal wealth (45× vs 29× for the average blend) with the least possible maintenance — about 3 trades a year and no fractional positions. The trade-off is buy-&-hold-level drawdowns in fast crashes. Best for long-horizon money you won't need to draw on soon.

How to execute it

  • Daily routine: after the close, check the two filters and set your QQQ weight per the rule above. Trade at the next session. Exits are rare — checking a few times a week is plenty.
  • Where to hold the asset: QQQ, or QQQM (same index, lower 0.15% fee). Do not use leveraged ETFs (TQQQ) — leverage breaks the risk profile.
  • Where to hold cash: a money-market fund, T-bills, or HYSA earning about the T-bill rate. The backtest credits cash the actual 3-month T-bill yield (averaging ~2% since 1999); that yield is a small part of the return edge, and the drawdown protection does not depend on it.
  • Account & tax: at ~3 trades/year this is quite tax-friendly, but a tax-advantaged account (IRA/Roth/401k) is still cleanest.
  • Discipline: follow the rule mechanically. The hard moments are selling when the market still feels fine and re-buying while it still feels scary.

What to expect / when it disappoints

  • It does not cushion sudden one-month crashes. In COVID it drew down −29% (same as buy & hold) because the trend filter stays "in" through a fast plunge. This is the price of staying invested for the extra return.
  • Its protection is for slow, grinding bears (2000–02, 2008, 2022) where at least one filter exits and stays out.
  • Edge is concentrated in those bears; in calm bull years it trails buy & hold on return, earning only the T-bill yield in cash during its rare exits.
  • In a relentless bull market you will trail buy & hold — the protection only pays off in the next deep bear.
